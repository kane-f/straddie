# Practical Info

## Things To Know

- Shops on the island are limited — pack sunscreen, a hat, insect
  repellent, and reusable water bottles, and dispose of rubbish properly.
- Telstra and Optus have the best mobile coverage, but signal is spotty
  outside townships — download maps offline.
- Check weather warnings and the National Parks and Wildlife website;
  coastal conditions can change quickly.
- Book ferries, accommodation, restaurants, and tours ahead — especially
  during school holidays and summer.

## Getting To North Stradbroke Island

| Option | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle ferry | Drive ~30km from Brisbane CBD to Toondah Harbour, Cleveland (~50 min via Old Cleveland Road). Book a vehicle ferry with SeaLink (~45 min crossing). Book ahead, especially in holidays. |
| Passenger ferry | Straddie Flyer or SeaLink (~20 min) from Cleveland. Straddie Flyer runs a free shuttle from Cleveland Station for public transport users. |
| Public transport | Cleveland line train (~1 hr) to Cleveland Station, then bus 258 to the ferry terminal (~15 min). A go card gets cheaper fares. |

!!! tip "Worth knowing"
    Double-check which ferry company you're booked with (Straddie Flyer vs.
    SeaLink) — and aim for an early ferry to make the most of the day.

## Getting Around Point Lookout

| Option | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Car | Most flexible. Point Lookout is 22km from Dunwich (ferry arrival point). Sealed roads connect all townships; a 4WD is only needed for beach driving. Fuel at Point Lookout Roadhouse (unleaded/diesel — no LPG on the island). |
| Bus | Stradbroke Island Buses connect Dunwich, Amity Point, and Point Lookout (all-day hop-on-hop-off). Meets most ferries — check timetables for early/late services. Credit card or cash only, no go cards. Dogs are no longer allowed on the buses. |
| Stradeasy Shuttle/Taxi | Private ferry-to-door transfers and group charters. Book by phone/text — 0408 193 685 for maxi-taxi. |
| Cycling | Dunwich to Point Lookout is 22km on sealed roads (mind narrow shoulders and trucks). Bike rentals at Manta Lodge or Straddie Super Sports. |
| Scooter | Scooters on Straddie offers rentals. |

!!! tip "Worth knowing"
    If you're relying on buses, plan around the afternoon schedule gaps —
    especially visiting Amity Point — so you don't miss the last ferry back.

## Four-Wheel-Drive Tracks

4WD driving is recreational only — the main roads are sealed. A **Vehicle
Access Permit** is required for beach driving (online, or at Minjerribah
Camping in Dunwich). Driving is prohibited one hour either side of high
tide to protect dunes and wildlife. Always carry a recovery kit and check
tide charts.

| Track | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Main Beach (24km) | Point Lookout south to Jumpinpin Channel. Main entry at George Nothling Drive. Hard sand at low tide; entry points can be churned up. Good for fishing, designated campsites, and accessing Keyhole Lakes. Watch for Queensland Police speed/breath checks. |
| Keyhole Lakes | Via Main Beach by 4WD. Freshwater lakes, good for a swim or picnic. No facilities — bring water and snacks, and go early to avoid crowds. |
| Flinders Beach | Shorter, beginner-friendly, with designated campsites (check availability via Minjerribah Camping). Watch for small creeks up to 30cm deep. |
| Tripod Track | Sandy, 4WD-only inland track linking East Coast Road (200m west of Beehive Road) to Alfred Martin Way. No permit needed. Single-lane with blind corners and a sandy hill climb — take it slow, yield to oncoming traffic, and avoid after heavy rain. Stop at Tripod Lookout for the view. |

## Support Local

Buy native plants at Point Lookout Bushcare Nursery for a unique souvenir.

## Respecting Quandamooka Culture

The Quandamooka people are the Traditional Owners of Minjerribah — see
[Things To Do → Arts, Culture & History](../things-to-do/index.md#arts-culture-history)
for galleries, the museum, and cultural centres.
